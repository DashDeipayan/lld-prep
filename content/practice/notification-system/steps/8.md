Answer each follow-up in 2–4 lines, naming **what changes**:

1. *"Let callers schedule a notification for a future time (e.g. a reminder in 2 hours)."*
2. *"Twilio is down. SMS must keep working."*
3. *"Users get too many 'someone liked your post' pushes. Batch them into a digest."*
4. *"Add WhatsApp as a channel."*

## 1. Scheduled notifications

- `Notification.scheduledAt`. Accept stores it and enqueues on a **delay queue** (or a `scheduled_notifications` table polled by a scheduler that moves due items to the normal queue).
- Allow `cancel(notificationId)` before it fires, which is a status `CANCELLED` the worker checks.
- Quiet hours reuse the same mechanism: "reschedule to 8 a.m. in the user's timezone".

## 2. Provider failover

- A `FailoverSender(primary, secondary)` **decorator** around the SMS adapters, plus a **circuit breaker** on the primary (open after N failures, half-open probe after a cool-down).
- The dispatcher and other channels don't change. Configuration decides the provider order, per country if costs differ.

## 3. Digests / batching

- A `DigestFilter` in the chain diverts low-priority, digest-eligible categories into a `DigestBuffer` keyed by `user:category`.
- A scheduler flushes buffers every N minutes or at M items, rendering one `likes_digest` template ("Ana and 12 others liked your post").
- The senders are untouched. This is a filter plus a scheduled job.

## 4. WhatsApp

- Add `Channel.WHATSAPP`, a `WhatsAppSender` adapter, WhatsApp templates (often pre-approved by the provider) and an address in `UserPreferences`. Then register the sender.
- That's it. The dispatcher, filters and retry logic don't change, which proves the Strategy/registry design from step 5.

> **Close with:** *"New channels are adapters, new rules are filters, new sending behaviour is a decorator, and status consumers are observers. The dispatcher core stayed the same through all four follow-ups."*
