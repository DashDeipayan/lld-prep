The interviewer said only *"Design a parking lot."* Before drawing anything, write down:

1. The **questions** you'd ask the interviewer (aim for 6–10).
2. The **functional requirements** you'd agree on, as a numbered list.
3. One or two **non-functional** requirements.
4. What you'll explicitly leave **out of scope**.

## Questions worth asking

- What vehicle types? Do some need bigger spots? *(bike, car, truck)*
- Spot sizes? Can a small vehicle use a big spot? *(yes, it can go up but never down)*
- How many floors, how many entry/exit gates? *(several of each)*
- How is the fee computed? Per hour? Per vehicle type? Minimum charge? *(per started hour, by vehicle type, minimum 1 hour)*
- How do we pick a spot: nearest to the entrance, or something else? *(nearest for now, and it should be swappable)*
- Do we need a display of free spots? *(yes, per size, kept live)*
- Payments, reservations, EV charging, monthly passes? *(not now, but keep it extensible)*
- Is this one lot in one process, or a distributed system? *(one lot, in memory)*

## Agreed functional requirements

1. A lot has several **floors**, each with **small / medium / large** spots.
2. Vehicles are **bike / car / truck**. Bike fits any spot, car fits medium or large, truck fits large only.
3. `park(vehicle)` picks a spot and issues a **ticket**. It fails if nothing fits or the plate is already inside.
4. `unpark(ticketId)` frees the spot and returns a **receipt** with the fee.
5. Fee = rate(vehicle type) × started hours, minimum 1 hour. **Pricing is swappable.**
6. Spot selection defaults to *lowest floor, then smallest fitting size*. **Selection is swappable.**
7. **Display boards** show free spots per size, updated live.

## Non-functional

- **Correct under concurrency**: many gates call park/unpark at once, and no spot is ever double-assigned.
- Park and unpark are fast, at most O(spots). Unpark should be O(1).

## Out of scope

Payment processing, reservations, persistence, user accounts, UI, and hardware such as barriers and cameras.

> **Interview tip:** read the requirements back as a numbered list and get a nod. You will refer back to "requirement 5" later when you justify the Strategy pattern.
