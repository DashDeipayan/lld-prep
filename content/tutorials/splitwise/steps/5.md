Five friends after a trip can have up to 10 pairwise debts. **Simplify** produces a short list of transfers that settles everyone.

## Greedy algorithm (the standard answer)

1. Compute every user's `balance`. Ignore zeros.
2. **Creditors** have balance > 0 and **debtors** have balance < 0.
3. Repeatedly take the **largest creditor** and the **largest debtor** (by absolute amount, ties → smaller user id), and transfer `min(credit, |debt|)` from the debtor to the creditor. Reduce both and drop whoever reaches 0.
4. Stop when everyone is at 0.

This yields **at most n − 1 transfers**. Finding the true minimum is NP-hard (it reduces to subset-sum), so say so, and say that the greedy approach is what Splitwise-like apps use.

`simplify()` returns a plan. It **doesn't** change any balances (the users still have to pay). Return `Transfer(from, to, amount)` records (Python: `Transfer(sender, receiver, amount)`, since `from` is a keyword) in the order the algorithm produces them.

## Example

**B** paid 50 for A, and **C** paid 50 for B. That's a chain: A owes B, and B owes C. Balances: A −50, B 0, C +50. Simplified: **one** transfer, A → C 50.
